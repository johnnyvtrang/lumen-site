Why lenses yellow

Fixed, not wiped over.

Headlight lenses are polycarbonate with a thin factory hard coat that blocks UV. Sun breaks that coat down, the plastic underneath oxidizes, and the lens goes hazy, then yellow.

Store kits take off some haze, but without a multi-stage machine polish the lens is left finely scratched, so it clouds again sooner. At Salt Lake's elevation the sun is stronger than at sea level, so factory coatings tend to break down sooner here. I machine-correct every lens through three polishing stages and seal it with a ceramic UV coating. A new headlight assembly often costs several hundred dollars per side, and $1,000 or more on many luxury cars.

Questions

Headlight questions.

Why not just use a headlight kit from the store?

Kits take off some haze, but without a multi-stage machine polish the lens is left finely scratched, so it clouds again sooner. I machine-correct the lens through three polishing stages, apply a ceramic UV coating, and back it with a 1-year clarity warranty — if it yellows or clouds within a year, I re-polish and re-coat it free.

How long does headlight restoration take?

About an hour for Polish, and 60–90 minutes for Restore, which adds wet-sanding for heavily yellowed lenses. It's done in your driveway or garage; headlight-only jobs just need an outlet.

What does the 1-year clarity warranty cover?

If a lens I restored yellows or clouds within 12 months, I re-polish and re-coat it free. It excludes impact damage, cracks and moisture inside the housing, so please text a photo of each lens when you book.

Can you polish faded emblems and badges?

Yes. Faded or cloudy chrome and plastic emblems, front and rear, are hand-polished and protected: $40 a set on its own, or $30 with any headlight service or any detail. If the plating is peeling or pitted, polish won't fix it, and I'll tell you before starting.
