Protected finishes · Wrap, matte & PPF

Matte paint, PPF & vinyl wrap care in Salt Lake City.

A wrap, matte paint or paint protection film is an investment that a normal car wash can undo in one visit: wax that leaves shiny patches on matte, a solvent that dries out vinyl, a pressure washer that lifts a film edge. I wash and protect these finishes with products chosen for each one, a test spot before anything new, and hand care at every edge — the same way every time.

Why it's different

Different surfaces, different chemistry.

Gloss paint is a hard clear coat that can be polished. A matte finish, a vinyl wrap and paint protection film are not — and the products that are fine on gloss paint are exactly the ones that damage them.

  • Matte and satin get their look from a texture that scatters light. Wax, gloss sprays and polish fill that texture in and leave shiny, blotchy patches — and on matte paint, polishing can't be undone.
  • Vinyl wraps are a thin plastic film held on by adhesive. Solvents and harsh degreasers dry the film out; water aimed at an edge can get under it and lift it.
  • PPF is a clear urethane film with a soft, self-healing top layer. Strong solvents can cloud or yellow it and attack the adhesive at the edges.
  • Matte PPF and matte wraps carry both risks at once: they can be dulled like matte and lifted like film.

That's why every finish gets its own wash, its own topper and its own technique. The chemistry behind pH and coatings, with sources, is on the science page.

What I do differently

Seven things, every visit.

  1. 01

    Ask, then follow the maker

    I ask who installed the wrap, film or coating, and for their care guidance. If the installer or coating maker has rules, I follow them — and some coating warranties need the installer's own annual inspection, so I'll tell you if yours does.

  2. 02

    Identify the finish

    Gloss or matte, paint or film, coated or not. A test rinse shows how water behaves on the surface: tight beads that roll off fast usually mean it's coated.

  3. 03

    Test spot first

    Any product new to your car goes on a hidden spot first, and I check it before going further. Bug and tar removers are always tested and soaked, never scrubbed.

  4. 04

    A wash chosen for the finish

    Every finish starts with a gentle pH-neutral foam and a two-bucket hand wash. Film gets a wax-free wash made for film; matte gets a matte-specific wash; a coated car gets its coating maker's wash or a pH-neutral one. No wash-and-wax, no gloss enhancers, no routine degreasers.

  5. 05

    Edges by hand

    The pressure washer stays at least 12 inches back with a wide 40° fan tip, and it's never aimed at an edge, seam or badge. Film edges, seams and wrap seams are washed by hand.

  6. 06

    Blower drying

    A filtered blower pushes water out of edges, seams and mirrors so it can't sit under the film or leave spots. On matte, any last water is blotted, never buffed.

  7. 07

    A topper matched to the finish

    A ceramic topper on coated paint and gloss film; a matte-safe detailer that adds no shine on matte paint, matte wraps and matte PPF. When a coating is booked, it's one its maker rates for that finish — never polished first.

At home

What not to do between visits.

Most damage to these finishes happens in a few minutes, with good intentions.

  • No automatic brush or soft-cloth car washes: the brushes scratch film and can catch an edge.
  • No wax (paste, liquid or spray), wash-and-wax or gloss-enhancing detail sprays on matte paint, a matte wrap or matte PPF.
  • Never polish, compound, clay or use a melamine sponge on matte — it burnishes shiny spots that don't come back out.
  • No solvent tar removers, degreasers or all-purpose cleaners near film edges or wrap seams.
  • Don't hold a pressure washer close, or aim it at an edge, seam, badge or a chip in the film.
  • Don't scrub bugs or bird droppings: soak them with your wash and lift them off.
  • Don't wash or let water dry in direct sun, and keep sprinklers pointed away from the car.

Aftercare

Keeping it right between washes.

  1. 01

    New film or wrap

    Follow your installer's wait before the first wash; the adhesive needs time to set at the edges.

  2. 02

    Wash gently, often

    A pH-neutral, wax-free wash (matte-specific on matte), two buckets and a soft mitt, out of the sun. Rinse from the top down.

  3. 03

    Same-day spot care

    Bird droppings, bugs and tree sap etch film and matte fast in Utah sun. Soak them with your wash and lift them off the same day.

  4. 04

    Dry without rubbing

    A blower, or a plush towel laid on and blotted. Don't buff matte.

  5. 05

    Renew the protection

    Toppers wear with washing. A Decon & Reseal every 6–12 months takes off iron fallout and hard-water minerals and renews the topper for your finish.

Questions

Wrap, matte & PPF questions.

How do you wash matte paint without making it shiny?

With a matte-specific wash, no wax, gloss sprays or polish, and no rubbing: a gentle foam, a soft two-bucket hand wash, then a blower and a blotting towel. Protection is a matte-safe detailer that adds no shine. Anything new is tried on a hidden spot first.

How do you wash PPF without lifting the edges?

The pressure washer stays at least 12 inches away with a wide 40° fan tip and is never aimed at an edge; edges and seams are washed by hand with a wax-free wash made for film, and the edges are blown dry so water can't sit under them. No strong solvents touch the film.

Is it safe to pressure wash a vinyl wrap?

Yes, at a distance and a wide angle, and never aimed at an edge, seam or chip in the film — that's how water gets under a wrap and lifts it. Close-in work around edges and badges is done by hand.

Can you polish or clay matte paint?

No, and I won't. Polishing and claying flatten the texture that makes matte look matte, and the shiny spots they leave can't be undone. Matte paint is cleaned chemically and gently, and protected with matte-safe products.

Can you ceramic coat PPF, a vinyl wrap or matte paint?

Yes, with a coating its maker rates for that finish (makers rate them up to 24 months to two years), from $629 on a sedan. There's no polishing: the coating goes on after a decon wash. It needs a garage I can work in so it cures out of the sun, wind and dust.

My installer gave me care instructions. Will you follow them?

Yes. Tell me who installed the film, wrap or coating and send their care guidance; I follow it, and if their warranty needs their own annual inspection, I'll tell you.

What should I never use on matte paint or a wrap?

Automatic brush washes, wax and wash-and-wax, gloss sprays, polish, clay, melamine sponges, and solvent tar removers or degreasers near film edges. Bugs and bird droppings should be soaked and lifted, not scrubbed.

How much does a wash for a wrapped, matte or PPF car cost?

On a sedan, the Refresh Wash is $119, the Decon & Reseal is $199 and the Protected Full Detail is $269. Larger vehicles cost more; the quote builder prices your exact vehicle.
