Lab Notes · Living with a coating

How long protection lasts in the Salt Lake Valley.

By Johnny Trang, founder · Updated November 2026 · 14 min read

Every coating we apply has a maker's rating, from about 6 weeks for a topper to up to five years for a two-layer paint coating, and how much of that rating a car actually gets depends on how it's driven, washed and parked. Below, each rating is quoted from the maker, next to Lumen's projection for three kinds of ownership in the Salt Lake Valley. Our projections are never longer than the maker's rating, and they are estimates, not warranties.

How to read the numbers

  • Maker rating. Quoted word for word from the maker's current product page, in the maker's own units. It is the maker's claim, not ours, and it assumes the maker's care instructions are followed.
  • Distance figures. Several makers rate a coating in time and distance (25kkm is 25,000 km, about 15,500 miles). We read whichever comes first as the limit, so every projection below stops at the maker's distance figure too.
  • Lumen projection. Our estimate for the Salt Lake Valley, never longer than the maker's rating. Where two makers' products can be used for one service, we project from the lower rating. Your job report names the product we used.
  • Cared for: maintenance washes as the maker recommends (pH-neutral, about every two weeks), an Annual Coating Service each year, garaged.
  • Typical: regular hand washes, parked outside.
  • Hard use: high miles (we draw the line at about 15,000 a year), frequent automatic washes, winter salt and mag chloride, trail use, sprinklers or hard water.
  • Interior coatings use the same three labels: cared for means garaged with seats wiped down with a pH-neutral cleaner; hard use means daily commuting with kids, pets, work clothes or wipes and cleaners outside the maker's pH range.
  • How we set the ranges. Cared for is roughly three-quarters to all of the rating, typical about half to three-quarters, hard use about a quarter to a half. They come from the makers' ratings and care conditions and the local conditions in this note, not from measurements on a fleet of cars. We'll revise them as our own job reports build a record.

Paint coatings

All three paint coatings share the same enemies here: abrasion from washing and road grit, strong chemistry, and minerals and road film that cover the coating so it looks worn before it is.

  • Lumen projection: 18–24 months cared for, 12–18 typical, 6–12 hard use, or about 15,500 miles, whichever comes first.
  • Miles matter here: at 12,000 miles a year, the distance figure arrives at about 15 months, before the time figure.
  • Shortens it: a single layer has no separate top layer to take the wear, so automatic washes, strong pre-soaks and gritty wash mitts wear the coating itself.

When a paint coating needs attention

  • Beading or sheeting fades after a proper wash. Usually minerals or road film are covering the coating; a decon is the first step, not a new coating.
  • The surface feels grabby under the back of your fingers on a clean, dry panel.
  • It stays clean less easily and dirt clings after a rinse, even after a decontamination.
  • Reapply only when a half-panel decon test shows nothing left to uncover. The three home tests.

What shortens protection here

  • Miles and highway grit. Several makers rate coatings by distance as well as time,1,2,3 and grit moved across a surface by brushes and wiping material causes the fine mars that build up with repeated washing.37
  • UV at altitude. The NOAA solar monitoring station near the Salt Lake City airport sits at 1,288 m, about 4,226 feet,35 and UV intensity increases with altitude.34 We found no published data that puts a number on how much this shortens a given coating, so we don't add one.
  • Winter road treatment. UDOT pre-treats roads with salt brine and uses white salt and higher-performance salts,32 about 236,000 tons of road salt a year by one report.33 The film it leaves holds grime and gets washed off more often. Mag chloride, road salt and your car.
  • Automatic washes. Coating makers tell owners to avoid them,30,31 brushes carry grit, and touchless washes use stronger chemistry to make up for no contact. Why frequent automatic washes wear paint down.
  • Harsh or high-pH soaps. Paint coatings we use list a pH tolerance of 2–11;1 leather and fabric coatings and the toppers list 4–9.19,29 Cleaners outside those ranges wear them faster.
  • Hard water and sprinklers. Salt Lake City's 2025 water report lists total hardness from 121 mg/L to as high as 487 mg/L by source.36 Minerals that dry on a coating cover it, and spots that bake on in the sun can etch. Hard-water spots in Utah.
  • Parking outside or under trees. More sun, and bird droppings, sap and pollen sit longer. One coating maker's care rules say not to leave bird droppings or other contaminants on the paint.30
  • Trail use. Brush, packed mud and grit scrub the lower panels and wheels, and mud left to dry holds moisture against them. Rock chips need film, not a coating. How to choose a coating.

What extends it

  • pH-neutral hand washes about every two weeks; one maker puts it at every two weeks or about 650 miles.30,31 How to wash a coated car.
  • The Coated Care Wash, a finish-safe hand wash that ends with a ceramic topper on a coated car.
  • A topper or ceramic spray between washes, from the coating's own maker when it's known.
  • The Annual Coating Service: decontamination (iron, tar and mineral), a gentle wash, inspection and photos, and a reseal with the coating maker's product. Coating care.
  • Garaging, and sprinklers pointed away from where the car is parked.
  • Drying with a blower or blotting with a plush towel instead of dragging one across the paint.

When to top up or reapply

Go by what the surface does, not only the calendar.

  • Beading or sheeting fades after a proper wash and rinse.
  • The surface feels grabby instead of slick on a clean, dry panel.
  • It stays clean less easily, and still does after a decontamination.
  • Glass: rain no longer clears at speed, or the wipers judder on clean glass.
  • Wheels: brake dust needs scrubbing instead of rinsing off.
  • Trim and headlights: gray, chalky plastic or a dull, hazy lens.
  • Interior: spills soak in instead of sitting on top.

If beading comes back after a decon, the coating was covered, not gone: a topper is enough. If it doesn't, it's time to reapply.

Ratings, estimates and warranties

  • Maker ratings assume the maker's care instructions are followed. Some say so in the rating itself ("when properly maintained").
  • Our projected ranges are estimates, not warranties. They describe what we expect, not what we promise.
  • Coating warranties are separate, and their terms are on the coatings page. Manufacturer-backed guarantees apply only through each brand's accredited installers.

Questions

How long does a ceramic coating last in Utah?

Our paint coatings are maker-rated up to 24 months (Essential), up to 36 months (Signature), and up to 50 months or up to five years, depending on the option (Reserve). In the Salt Lake Valley we project that a garaged car with maintenance washes and an Annual Coating Service gets roughly three-quarters to all of that; a car parked outside and hand washed, about half to three-quarters; a hard-used car, a quarter to a half. Those are our estimates, never longer than the maker's rating.

Do miles matter, or only years?

Miles matter. Several makers rate a coating in time and distance, for example up to 24 months or 25,000 km (about 15,500 miles). We treat whichever comes first as the limit, so at 12,000 miles a year that coating reaches its distance figure at about 15 months.

Is the projected range a warranty?

No. The maker's rating is the maker's claim, and our projection is an estimate of what to expect here. Coating warranty terms are listed separately on the coatings page.

How do I know it's time to top up or reapply?

Watch what the surface does: beading or sheeting fades after a proper wash, the paint feels grabby instead of slick, or it stays clean less easily even after a decontamination. If beading comes back after a decon, a topper is enough. If it doesn't, the coating is worn and it's time to reapply.

Will automatic car washes shorten my coating's life?

Yes. Coating makers tell owners to avoid them. Brushes carry grit across the surface, and touchless washes rely on stronger chemistry. If one is unavoidable in winter, touchless is the gentler choice.
