Lab Notes · Before you book

Questions to ask any detailer before you book.

By Johnny Trang, founder · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

Ask how they'll protect your paint, what the protection is rated for by its maker, who pays if something goes wrong, and what you'll get back at the end. Good answers are specific: a measurement, a maker's rating, a policy, a photo. Here are ten questions, why each one matters, and how we answer them.

Send every detailer you're considering the same photos and the same questions, so the answers are comparable. If you're pricing a ceramic coating, What ceramic coating costs in Salt Lake City has a shorter list just for coatings.

1. Do you measure paint depth before polishing?

Why it matters. Polishing works by removing clear coat, and clear coat is usually only about 35–50 µm thick. One gauge maker reports that most carmakers advise removing no more than about 8 µm, and that carmakers now specify measuring paint before and after any wet sanding or buffing.1 The collision-repair training body I-CAR warns that taking off too much clear can cause hazing, discoloration, peeling and finish failure.2 You can't see how much is left by eye.

How we answer. On every correction and coating job, every panel is gauged before any machine work and again as we go. Thin paint means minimal correction only, and we tell you before we start. You get the readings panel by panel. More on gauging.

2. Which product, and what does its maker rate it for?

Why it matters. "Lasts years" is a claim; a maker's rating is a number you can check. Coating makers publish a rated life on the product page, sometimes with a mileage and the pH range the coating tolerates.3 Real life depends on how the car is driven, washed and stored, so a rating is a ceiling, not a promise.

How we answer. Every durability number on our site is the maker's own rating, said as a rating. We name the product in your job record, along with its lot number and the warranty end date.

3. What prep and which correction grade are in this price?

Why it matters. A coating locks in whatever is under it, so two prices for "a coating" can be two very different jobs: one with a polish first, one without.

How we answer. We price the coating and the correction separately, so you can see each one. On gloss paint the minimum is a Grade I polish. Every coating gets the same prep: washed, decontaminated, clayed, panel-wiped and inspected. Your price is confirmed after inspection and a written quote. The coatings and what's included.

4. Are you insured, and for what?

Why it matters. Insurance brokers describe two separate pieces: general liability for things like a slip-and-fall, and garage keepers coverage for accidental damage to a customer's vehicle in the business's care, custody or control.4 Ask which ones a detailer carries, and ask what isn't covered.

How we answer. Yes, we're insured. If something is damaged during our work, we tell you right away, document it with photos, and handle it through repair or insurance, as our service agreement sets out.

5. Is my finish coated, PPF, wrapped or matte, and what changes because of it?

Why it matters. The right products depend on the finish. A film maker's care guide says to keep a pressure washer at least 3 feet from the film and away from its edges, which can lift, and not to use waxes or polishes with dyes, abrasives or solvents on it.5 A wrap maker's maintenance bulletin says to test products on a small, hidden area first, to use no wax with petroleum distillates or abrasives, and warns that pressure washing can lift wrap edges.6 Coatings have a pH range they tolerate.3

How we answer. We ask whether it's coated, PPF, wrapped or matte, and who did it, then look for film edges, sheen and how water beads. When we're unsure we do a test spot with the gentlest product first. Matte is never polished, and film edges are washed by hand with the pressure washer kept back. How we care for wrapped, matte and PPF cars.

6. What happens if you miss something?

Why it matters. Even careful work misses a streak now and then, often one you only see in different light the next morning. A clear policy tells you what happens next, and for how long.

How we answer. Not happy? Tell us within 48 hours and we'll come back and make it right. Send photos and we reply the same day; the fix costs you nothing.

7. How long will you spend on my car?

Why it matters. Most of what you pay for is time on the car. A price with an hour figure next to it is easier to compare than a price alone.

How we answer. Our prices come from a model of the hands-on minutes each area of a vehicle takes. On a sedan, a Refresh takes about 1 hour inside or out and a Full Radiance takes about 6 hours; the quote builder shows the time for your exact vehicle, and your job record notes the hours on the car.

8. Will I get before and after photos?

Why it matters. Photos taken before any work show the car's condition, chips and scratches included, so nobody has to remember. The after set shows what changed.

How we answer. Yes, on every job at every level. The before set covers all four sides, the three-quarters, each wheel, the interior and every damage spot; the after set repeats the angles. You get them by text the same day.

9. Do you inspect the work under lights before handing it back?

Why it matters. High spots, streaks and missed dressing hide in daylight and show in direct light.

How we answer. Yes. The last pass on every job, at every level, is under detailing lights, against a written checklist: glass, high spots and residue, dressing, jambs, wheels, personal items back where they were. See a sample job report.

10. What do I need to do afterwards, and what does the warranty ask of me?

Why it matters. Coatings and films need cure time, and many warranties depend on how the car is washed and maintained. Get the terms in writing before you pay.

How we answer. We tell you the dry and cure times out loud at handover, kids and pets included. Coated cars get a care card, a free check-in about 30 days after installation, and a free inspection within 30 days of each anniversary, which keeps the coating warranty in force.

Questions

Should I ask every detailer the same questions?

Yes. Send each one the same photos and the same questions, so you're comparing the same job. Specific answers, like a measurement, a maker's rating or a written policy, are easier to compare than general ones.

Does a regular detail need paint measured?

Gauging matters when clear coat is about to be removed, which means machine polishing and correction. We gauge every panel on correction and coating jobs, and on any car where thin paint is suspected.

Is a maker's durability rating a promise?

No. It's what the maker rates the product for. Real life depends on how the car is driven, washed and stored. That's why we quote ratings as ratings.

What do I get back at the end of a job?

Before and after photos by text the same day, the dry and cure times said out loud, and care notes for your service. Correction and coating jobs add panel-by-panel paint readings, 50/50 photos and, for a coating, the warranty end date on your receipt.
