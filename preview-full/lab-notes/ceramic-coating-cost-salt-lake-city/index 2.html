Lab Notes · Coating cost

What ceramic coating costs in Salt Lake City (2026).

By Johnny Trang, owner · Updated November 2026 · 12 min read

In the Salt Lake Valley, shops that publish their prices charge about $450 to $1,650 to ceramic coat a sedan in 2026, and most multi-year packages with a light polish land between about $700 and $1,200. The price moves with the size of the vehicle, how much paint correction it needs, and how long the maker rates the coating to last; at Lumen, a sedan's coating is $500 to $700, plus the correction grade it goes over, from $399.

Two things stand out. First, the low end of the range is mostly short-rated coatings or packages that leave correction out. Second, the top end is mostly about the rated life on the label and how much correction is folded in, not about the vehicle.

For a wider view, one price index read 3,354 prices from 1,032 shops' own websites in October 2026. The middle price was $995, half of all prices fell between $600 and $1,400, and the middle for a sedan was $990.9 Its September report didn't list Salt Lake City, because it only publishes a metro once at least 10 shops there post a price; two western neighbors it did list were Denver, with a $1,200 median, and Phoenix, at $799.10 The Salt Lake numbers above sit inside that national band.

What moves the price

Every shop I read names the same few drivers. Here is what each one does to the number, and why.

Vehicle size

More panels take more product and more hours. Across 206 shops that post the same job at several sizes, an SUV ran about 14% over a car and a full-size truck about 25% over.9 The local pages above follow the same pattern: the step from a sedan to a three-row SUV is usually $100 to $400. My coating prices are per vehicle size too. The prices in this note are for a sedan; the coating advisor shows the price for your vehicle.

Paint condition and correction grade

This is usually the largest swing. A coating locks in whatever is under it, so swirled or etched paint is polished first, and polishing is hours of machine work.2,11 One directory's shops open paint correction at a typical $395 on its own.11 A local installer adds $300 for a two-step correction and $600 for a three-step on a sedan.6 A garage-kept car a few months old might need a light polish. A daily driver with two years of automatic-wash marks can need a multi-step correction that doubles the job.11

I gauge every panel before I polish, because clear coat is only a few dozen microns thick and the reading sets how much can safely come off. Why that matters is in Paint depth gauging.

The coating's rated life

Shops sell each durability tier as a separate product. In the national index, the middle price ran about $375 for coatings rated under a year, $599 for one year, $760 for two, $950 for three to four, $1,200 for five to seven, and $1,500 for eight years and up.9 Read those year counts as the maker's rating under the maker's conditions. Real life depends on how the car is driven, washed and parked. How to match the rating to how long you'll keep the car is in How to choose a ceramic coating.

Prep

Before any coating, the paint has to be washed, decontaminated (iron and tar removed), clayed, and wiped down so nothing sits between the coating and the clear coat. That's hours of work whether the coating is rated for one year or ten. A package that seems cheap often has less of this in it.2

What gets coated

Most starting prices cover the painted panels only.12 Glass, wheels and trim are add-ons at some shops3 and included at others.4 When you compare two quotes, line up the surfaces first.

Mobile, shop or garage

A coating has to go on in shade, out of the wind and dust, and stay dry for the maker's cure window. Shops do this in their bay. Mobile installers usually ask for your garage; one local page says a suitable garage or an arranged drop-off is required.3 Mobile isn't automatically cheaper or dearer: the local mobile packages above run from $450 to $1,699. I apply paint coatings in your garage when you have one. Without a garage, I use coatings made for outdoor application and plan the day around the weather.

Maintenance over the years

The price on the day isn't the whole cost. Many maker warranties depend on a yearly inspection or service, and one local installer lists annual maintenance from $150 to keep its warranty active.1 My Annual Coating Service is from $199 on a sedan: an inspection, a decon wash, iron removal and a booster top-up. Add that to the coating when you compare a two-year rating with a five-year one. In Utah, the routine matters more than usual: hard-water spots and mag chloride both work on a coating's top surface, which is covered in Hard-water spots and Mag chloride and road salt.

Why a $400 coating is a different job

You've probably seen ads on Facebook and Marketplace for a "ceramic coating" at $300 to $400. Some of those are honest jobs that fit what they are. Most are a different product and a different amount of work than a multi-year install, and the price tells you where the difference is.

  • The product. "Ceramic coating" covers everything from a one-year spray-on package to a decade-rated system over two days of correction, with the same words on the invoice.11 In the national index, coatings rated under a year sat around $250 to $450.9 A $400 price usually lines up with that class, not with the multi-year tiers.
  • The hours. One automotive guide puts a professional exterior coating at 15 to 20 hours of work, and a do-it-yourself exterior at about $200 to $400 in total.12 A $400 job can't pay for both the product and that many hours, so something is shorter: usually the decon and the polishing.
  • The prep. Local installers say plainly that bargain specials tend to skip paint prep, so the coating fails early or seals in the swirls.2 A coating over swirled paint keeps the swirls for as long as it lasts.
  • The product you can't check. If the ad doesn't name the coating, you can't look up what its maker rates it for, what pH it tolerates, or how long it has to stay dry. You're trusting a durability number with nothing behind it.
  • The warranty. Some makers only let certified installers register their warranty.11 A warranty the installer writes themselves is only as good as that installer.

If you'll own the car about a year, a short-rated product can be a fair choice. It just isn't the same thing as a multi-year coating over corrected paint, and it shouldn't be priced as if it were.

Red flags

  • A firm price without seeing the paint. Correction is the biggest variable, so a shop that prices your car without looking at it has priced somebody else's car.11 A starting price is normal; a final one sight unseen isn't.
  • No product name. You should be able to look up the maker's own page for what goes on your car.
  • "Permanent," "lifetime" or "never wash again." Coatings wear, and every maker I've read asks for regular washing and care. One local installer says plainly that a coating isn't maintenance-free or scratch-proof.3
  • "9H" as the main reason to buy. It's a pencil-hardness result, useful for ranking coatings in a lab, and it says little about how a coating holds up to your washing. The details are in How to choose a ceramic coating.
  • No word about correction. If the quote doesn't say what happens to existing swirls, they'll probably be under the coating.
  • Coating in a driveway in full sun or wind, with no plan. Heat, dust and early water all work against a coating while it cures.
  • Pressure. A deal that ends tonight, or a deposit before anyone has looked at the car.
  • No aftercare in writing. You should leave knowing the cure time, what soap to use, and what keeps the warranty in force.

Questions to ask any installer

  1. Which exact coating, and what does its maker rate it for? Time, mileage if given, and the pH range it tolerates.
  2. What prep and which correction grade are in this price? Ask what defects it removes, and what it can't safely remove.9
  3. Do you measure paint depth before polishing? Re-sprayed panels and thin clear coat change what's safe.
  4. Which surfaces are coated? Paint only, or glass, wheels and trim too.
  5. Where is it applied, and how long does it cure before I can drive in rain or wash it?
  6. What does the warranty cover, who backs it, and what does it ask of me? Registration, a yearly service, and what that service costs.
  7. What could change the price after you inspect it, and will you ask me first? Get the agreed package in writing.9

Send each installer the same vehicle details and photos, so the answers are comparable.9

Questions

How much is a ceramic coating in Salt Lake City?

On a sedan, local shops that publish prices list about $450 to $1,650 in 2026, and most multi-year packages with a light polish fall between about $700 and $1,200. SUVs and trucks usually run $100 to $400 more. Stacked multi-layer packages go higher.

Is paint correction included in a coating price?

It depends on the shop. Some include a one-step polish, some fold in full correction on their top tier, and some leave correction out entirely. Ask which correction grade is in the price before you compare two quotes. I price them separately so you can see both.

Why are some coatings only $300 or $400?

Usually because the product is rated for a year or less, the prep and polishing are shorter, or both. A multi-year install over corrected paint takes many hours of work, and a $400 price can't cover that and a long-rated product at the same time.

Is a longer-rated coating worth the extra cost?

If you'll keep the car for years and stick with the care routine, a longer rating can cost less per year. If you'll sell it in two years, a shorter rating usually fits. The maker's rating is an "up to" figure under its own conditions, not a promise for every car.

How much more does an SUV or truck cost?

Across many shops, an SUV runs about 14% over a car and a full-size truck about 25% over. Locally, the step from a sedan to a three-row SUV is usually $100 to $400. My coating advisor shows the price for your vehicle.

What does a coating cost to maintain?

Regular hand washes with a pH-neutral soap, plus whatever yearly service the warranty asks for. Mine asks only for a free yearly inspection; the Annual Coating Service, from $199 on a sedan, is there if the coating needs a deep clean. When you compare warranties, check what the yearly visit costs.

Is a mobile coating as good as one done in a shop?

It can be, if the coating goes on in shade, out of the wind and dust, and stays dry for its cure window. That's why I apply paint coatings in your garage when you have one, and plan around the weather when you don't.
