Local notes

Detailing in Farmington.

What we see most on cars here, and what we do about it.

  1. 01

    Coating day or wind day

    That May 2024 warning called for sustained winds of 30 to 40 mph, gusts to 65, and difficult travel for high-profile vehicles on I-15, US-89 and Legacy Parkway.1 A ceramic coating has to cure clean, so we apply one outdoors only when the maker allows it and the day is calm enough. On a wind day the work moves into a garage or covered space, or to another date at no fee.

    Paint correction & ceramic coating

  2. 02

    Secondary water almost everywhere

    Farmington's 2021 water conservation plan says every residence is required to have both culinary and secondary service, and secondary water reaches over 95% of the city, supplied by Benchland Irrigation and Weber Basin.2 So most driveways sit near a lawn on its own sprinkler system. A windshield or all-glass ceramic coating helps overspray wipe away, once the existing spots are off.

    Glass & wheel coatings

  3. 03

    Five wells and a Weber Basin contract

    The same plan lists five city wells for drinking water, plus a long-term contract for Weber Basin water.2 Weber Basin puts its own water at 10 to 18 grains per gallon;3 the city's wells have no published figure we could reach. So we treat Farmington tap water as hard: every wash ends with a touchless blower dry and plush towels, and nothing is left to dry on the paint by itself.

    Mobile detailing

Questions

Farmington questions.

Is there a travel fee to Farmington?

Yes, a flat $25 per trip. Lumen members pay half.

Can you coat my car in my Farmington driveway?

Yes, when the coating's maker allows outdoor application and the day is calm and dry. Otherwise it goes in your garage or a covered space, or by drop-off. The paint gets a full wash, decon, clay and panel wipe first; correction is optional.

Is my secondary water the same as my tap water?

No. Farmington's tap water comes from city wells and Weber Basin; secondary water is a separate system run by Benchland Irrigation or Weber Basin, and it's meant for lawns, not drinking or washing cars.
