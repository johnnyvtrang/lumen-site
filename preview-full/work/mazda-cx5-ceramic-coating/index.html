Recent work · Ceramic coating

A white CX-5, coated for easy cleaning.

By Johnny Trang, founder · November 2026 · 2021 Mazda CX-5 · Salt Lake Valley

A 2021 CX-5 parked next to a sprinkler came in with heavy water spots and a contaminated clear coat. We took the spots off least aggressive first: wash, then an acid water-spot remover, then polishing only on the small areas where the spots had etched into the clear coat. Then the chrome trim and emblems were polished, stains came out of the Alcantara seat inserts, the steering wheel was deep-cleaned, the piano-black trim was lightly polished, and the paint got one layer of ceramic coating.

Time on the car
5–6 h
Correction
spot polish, etched areas only
Coating layers
1
Maker rating
up to 5 yr

What we found

  • Water spots, heavy. The car is parked next to a sprinkler, so the same panels get wet and dry again and again. A drying drop leaves its minerals behind in a ring, and each re-wetting adds more.1
  • A contaminated clear coat. The paint itself was in okay shape, but bonded deposits sat across the surface before any work started.
  • Stains on the Alcantara seat inserts.
  • A steering wheel with a buildup of lotion and hand oils.
  • Piano-black interior trim with light marks.

The owner's goal was easy cleaning, and she chose not to have the whole car polished. A coating seals the paint as it is, so with her OK we polished only the few spots that needed it to come clean.

What we did and why

Water spots come off in stages, and the order matters. Each step up the ladder removes more, and the last step, polishing, removes clear coat that doesn't grow back. So we start at the bottom and climb only as far as the spots force us to (the full ladder).

  1. Wash and decontaminate. Foam, a two-bucket hand wash, then iron and tar removers and clay where the paint still felt rough. You can't judge spots through dirt, and grit left on the paint would scratch it in the steps that follow.
  2. Acid water-spot remover for the mineral deposits. Most hard-water minerals are carbonates, and an acid reacts with carbonate to make carbon dioxide and water, so the calcium dissolves and rinses away.2 It went on cool panels in small sections, was never allowed to dry, and was rinsed off right away. Ours is a detailing-grade remover; we never use hydrofluoric or bifluoride products.
  3. Spot polishing, only where the spots had etched. Once the minerals were gone, a few rings still showed under the light as dull outlines. Those had eaten into the clear coat, and no wash or acid removes an etch; it can only be levelled by polishing.3 Polishing does that by removing clear coat around the ring. Most factory clear coat is about 35–50 µm thick, and one gauge maker reports that most carmakers advise removing no more than about 8 µm of it.4 That's why only the etched areas were polished, and only after the milder steps had failed there.
  4. Chrome trim and emblems, polished by hand. Light pressure, by hand rather than by machine, after testing a corner. If the towel picks up color, the plated finish itself is coming off, so we stop.
  5. Alcantara seat inserts: stains removed by hand. We extracted the stains manually, by hand rather than with a machine. Alcantara's maker says to work a stain from the outside edge into the center so it doesn't spread, never to pour cleaner directly onto it, to avoid rubbing, and to use a white cloth or a well-wrung sponge.5 It also says not to steam it.6 A carmaker's owner's manual warns against disturbing the material's nap and says to brush it back into line once it's dry.7 Working by hand keeps the moisture and pressure low enough to protect that nap.
  6. Steering wheel, deep-cleaned. Hand lotion and skin oils build up on a wheel rim that's held every day. Mazda's own owner's manual says leather that comes into contact with grease or oil, such as hand cream, should be cleaned off right away, because left in place it can cause premature wear, mold or stains.8 The rim and spokes were cleaned with a gentle cleaner on a towel and wiped clean; the center pad, an airbag cover, gets a water-damp microfiber only.
  7. Piano-black trim, lightly polished. High-gloss black trim is easy to mark: a coatings-industry article notes that piano-black surfaces show fingerprints and are highly susceptible to scratches.9 The thin glossy layer leaves little to work with, so it got a light finishing polish only, with light pressure, to take out the fine marks.
  8. Panel wipe, then one layer of ceramic coating. Small sections, levelled and buffed, checked for high spots under the light. The coating goes on as a liquid and cures into a thin, glass-like film.

Coating on this job

A Gtechniq ceramic coating, one layer. Gtechniq rates it for "up to five years of protection when properly maintained".

What it took

About 5–6 hours on the car, start to finish. One layer of ceramic coating, maker-rated up to 5 years when properly maintained. The rating is the maker's, and it depends on care.

What the photo shows: water pulls off the coated hood in streaks and leaves small, tight beads behind. That's the water behavior that makes a coated car easier to clean. Honest limits: a coating doesn't stop rock chips, and the panels that weren't polished are sealed as they were. A coating also doesn't stop new water spots from forming if a sprinkler keeps wetting the car; it makes them easier to wash off while they're fresh.

Care

  • The sprinkler: park out of its reach, or turn the head so it doesn't hit the car. That one change does more against spots than any product.
  • Indoors and dry for 12 hours after application, per the maker.
  • pH-neutral soap, two buckets, and dry the car after washing instead of letting it air-dry.
  • No wax and no automatic brush washes; touchless only if a winter wash can't be avoided (why).
  • Alcantara: dust it with a soft brush or dry cloth, and wipe it now and then with a slightly damp white cotton cloth, per its maker.6

The full routine is in How to wash a ceramic-coated car.
